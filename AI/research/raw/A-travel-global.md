## Problems

### P-A-01 — Group-trip money: the organizer fronts the cash, holds the risk, and chases debts
- **Who is affected:** The 1–2 friends who organize and pre-pay group trips. Secondarily, the friendships involved.
- **Trigger:** Booking a non-refundable shared item (villa/Airbnb, van, tour) that needs one card, before everyone has committed.
- **Proximate cause:** One person pays the full amount. The others "owe" informally, and some delay, ghost or drop out.
- **Possible root cause (mechanism):** Commitment and payment are separated in time. Platforms (Airbnb, hotels) take one payer, and ledger apps (Splitwise) record debt but do not *collect* or *escrow* it. Nothing turns "I'm in" into a financial commitment. *(Inference.)*
- **Current behavior / workaround:** Non-refundable deposits and "in-or-out" cutoff dates (ES-A-02). Splitwise ledgers (ES-A-01). Bank-transfer screenshots in group chat. Threatening to cancel the booking.
- **Harm / consequence:** Organizers absorb unpaid shares. One reported Reddit case: about $1,200 fronted for a 5-person rental with ~$240 shares unpaid (news rewrite, snippet). Friendships strain.
- **Current solutions & where they fail:** Splitwise tracks but doesn't collect, and its free tier capped daily expenses in Dec 2023, which hurts trip-burst logging (ES-A-01). Deposit advice relies on social pressure. No escrow-style "pledge before booking" mechanism was found in this scan *(absence not exhaustively verified)*.
- **Who benefits / loses:** Benefits: booking platforms (one payer, no friction), Splitwise (paywall conversion). Loses: the organizer.
- **Frequency / severity:** 74% of group trips are planned by 1–2 people, and 82.5% of organizers felt a psychological burden (Skyscanner, n=100 Korean travelers, opened, *single source, small sample*). Group trips ran 15.6% over budget on average (same source).
- **Problem type:** Reported.
- **WTP signal:** Splitwise Pro sells at US$5/mo / $40/yr, and the paywall drew complaints. That points to WTP among heavy users and resistance among occasional ones (opened, single source). Klook: >80% of Taiwanese planners value their planning time at ≥NT$1,600 (US$50)/hr (opened, vendor survey).
- **Evidence Status:** FACT (organizer burden numbers, Splitwise limit as of Dec 2023). HYPOTHESIS (that collection or commitment is the core gap). **Confidence:** med.
- **Sources:** https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10779087 (2026-06-10, opened); https://www.itvoice.in/splitwise-has-introduced-restrictions-on-the-number-of-free-expenses-users-can-add (2023-12-30, opened); https://www.buzzfeed.com/michaelabramwell/friend-group-trip-payment-drama-reddit-aita (snippet).
- **Uncertainty:** Current Splitwise limits are unverified. Korean sample n=100. No Thai-specific data. In Thailand, PromptPay QR transfers may already lower collection friction *(inference)*.
- **Build-feasibility (HYPOTHESIS):** High. A web page with "pledge link + deadline + per-person PromptPay QR + paid/unpaid board" is buildable in ~1.5 days using free tools (static site + free DB). Real escrow is NOT feasible (licensing).

### P-A-02 — Group trip *decision* coordination eats 10–40+ hours and falls on one person
- **Who is affected:** Group trip planners, especially in Asian markets (Taiwan, Korea data).
- **Trigger:** Picking dates, budget and activities among 3+ people.
- **Proximate cause:** Scattered chat threads. Members who say "anything is fine" but later object (49.2% of complaints about parents as companions were "I don't care, anything is fine", Skyscanner).
- **Possible root cause (mechanism):** No shared decision structure. Preference elicitation happens in unstructured chat, and the person who cares most absorbs all the coordination cost (a free-rider dynamic). *(Inference.)*
- **Current behavior / workaround:** One person does it. Polls in LINE or WhatsApp. Shared docs/sheets. AI planners (Mindtrip Group Chat, ES-A-03).
- **Harm:** ~75% across 10 markets said planning a weeklong group trip took >10 hours. In Taiwan, 39% spent >40 hours, and 51% named schedule coordination the hardest task (Klook, n=2,725). Leads to organizer burnout.
- **Current solutions & where they fail:** Planning startups repeatedly fail to monetize or raise funding (Tripnotes; ES-A-14). Planning is high-effort but low-frequency, so subscription is hard. AI planners optimize the itinerary, not group consensus *(inference)*.
- **Who benefits / loses:** OTAs benefit when one person books for all. The organizer loses.
- **Frequency/severity:** As above (Klook opened, vendor survey, single source. Skyscanner opened, single source).
- **Problem type:** Reported.
- **WTP signal:** Self-valued time ≥US$50/hr (Taiwan, Klook). There is no direct evidence anyone pays for group planning. Failed startups suggest weak WTP (ES-A-14).
- **Evidence Status:** FACT (survey numbers). HYPOTHESIS (mechanism). **Confidence:** med.
- **Sources:** https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/news/6419113 (2026-08-11, opened); https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10779087 (2026-06-10, opened); https://skift.com/2024/01/02/tripnotes-a-buzzy-chatgpt-based-trip-planner-was-acquired-and-shut-down/ (2024-01-02, opened).
- **Uncertainty:** Both surveys are vendor-run (Klook and Skyscanner sell travel). Monetization is the biggest risk.
- **Build-feasibility (HYPOTHESIS):** Medium-high. A date/budget poll page is easy but already commoditized (polls exist in chat apps). Novelty would need a mechanism such as linking the decision to the P-A-01 commitment.

### P-A-03 — Ground-transport overcharging in Thailand (taxi meter refusal, tuk-tuk/boat touts)
- **Who is affected:** Foreign tourists in Bangkok, Phuket and Pattaya. Locals too. Honest drivers lose reputation.
- **Trigger:** Hailing a taxi or tuk-tuk at tourist hotspots or airports, or at night.
- **Proximate cause:** Drivers refuse the meter and quote flat fares. Tourists don't know the fair price.
- **Possible root cause (mechanism):** Information asymmetry about prices. Weak enforcement ("systemic… weak law enforcement and a culture of impunity", ATTA). Repeat-game reputation is missing because tourists are one-shot customers. Fares that drivers consider too low (the government is researching fare levels) push them toward flat-rate quoting.
- **Current behavior / workaround:** Switch to Grab/Bolt (more expensive but transparent; ES-A-05). Use the Grab price as a negotiation anchor (ES-A-16). 1584 hotline (ES-A-06).
- **Harm:** Bangkok is among the top 4 cities for tourist scams, and 48% of its tourist fraud cases involve taxis or rental cars (Mastercard Economics Institute, May 2025). Scam cases rose 18% in the summer season and 28% in the 2024 winter holidays. Brand damage spreads via reviews in HK, Taiwan, Malaysia and Singapore. Some Chinese travelers have shifted to Vietnam (ANN, causal link asserted by the article, not proven).
- **Complaints:** 10,687 taxi complaints to the 1584 hotline in Oct 2023–Feb 2024: refusal 2,291, no meter 1,054, meter discrepancy 438, overcharging 503.
- **Current solutions & where they fail:** Grab covers cars only, costs more, and taxi lobbies contest its airport access. The hotline is reactive and tourist-unfriendly. No benchmark exists for tuk-tuks, boats or pier transfers.
- **Who benefits / loses:** Benefits: rule-bending drivers, tout networks, "pier cartels" (single source, unverified). Loses: tourists, compliant drivers, Thailand's tourism brand.
- **Problem type:** Observed (complaint data) + Reported.
- **WTP signal:** Tourists already pay Grab's premium for transparency (qualitative, ANN, opened). No sized number.
- **Evidence Status:** FACT. The 48% figure comes from two opened outlets (ANN 2025-05-26, Pattaya Mail 2025-05-22), but both cite ONE primary report (Mastercard), so it is *single primary source*. The complaint counts are single source (ANN). **Confidence:** high (that the problem exists); med (the numbers).
- **Sources:** https://asianews.network/?p=204969 (2025-05-26, opened); https://www.pattayamail.com/thailandnews/bangkok-named-among-worlds-worst-cities-for-tourist-scams-report-warns-501832 (2025-05-22, opened); https://asianews.network/?p=140074 (2024-03-25, opened).
- **Uncertainty:** The Mastercard primary report was not found or opened. Tourist-side Reddit evidence was not retrieved (searches with site:reddit.com returned no Reddit pages).
- **Build-feasibility (HYPOTHESIS):** High for a "fair price check" web page (crowd-reported or curated fare ranges per route plus a Thai/English script card to show the driver), using free hosting. Data cold-start is the main risk, not the code.

### P-A-04 — Lookalike "official" sites charge for free government travel forms (Thailand TDAC and e-visas)
- **Who is affected:** Every foreign arrival to Thailand. The TDAC digital arrival card has been mandatory since 2025.
- **Trigger:** Searching "Thailand arrival card" before a flight.
- **Proximate cause:** Paid ads and SEO lookalike sites rank above or near the official site and charge a "processing fee".
- **Possible root cause (mechanism):** Search and ad platforms monetize the intent. Governments don't advertise. Travelers can't tell official from unofficial (information asymmetry). Low enforcement across borders.
- **Current behavior / workaround:** Government warnings (May 2025 and again Oct 2025 means the problem persists). Hotline reporting (ES-A-07).
- **Harm:** Reported fees of US$10 (May 2025) up to US$20–90 (Oct 2025 Immigration figure). Personal and passport data exposure. One Trustpilot account (unverified) describes being charged US$239.97 against an agreed US$60. A claim that ≥10% of arrivals used such sites is secondhand (Blick citing Bangkok Post). Not verified.
- **Current solutions & where they fail:** Warnings don't reach travelers at the moment of search. They are reactive, and new domains appear.
- **Who benefits / loses:** Benefits: scam operators, ad platforms. Loses: travelers, Thailand's image.
- **Problem type:** Reported.
- **WTP signal:** Inverse signal. Travelers *already pay* US$10–90 for something free, which shows they will pay for "certainty I did it right" *(inference)*.
- **Evidence Status:** HYPOTHESIS. Several consistent news snippets plus a government page exist, but the Khaosod fetch was blocked (403) and others were not opened. **Confidence:** med.
- **Sources:** https://www.malaymail.com/news/world/2025/05/01/thailand-warns-travellers-of-fake-digital-arrival-card-website-charging-us10-for-free-entry-form/175225 (2025-05-01, snippet); https://www.khaosodenglish.com/tourism/2025/10/17/thailand-warns-tourists-of-fake-arrival-card-websites-charging-fees/ (2025-10-17, 403); https://www.mots.go.th/mots_en/news/3764 (snippet); https://ie.trustpilot.com/review/thailandtouristvisaok.com (snippet, 2026).
- **Uncertainty:** Scale is unknown, and the 10% claim is unverified.
- **Build-feasibility (HYPOTHESIS):** High. A free "official-link checker / pre-arrival checklist for Thailand" page is trivial to build. But it competes for the same search traffic, and monetization conflicts with trust (charging for it resembles the scam).

### P-A-05 — Flight-disruption compensation: valid claims are rejected and intermediaries take 25–50%
- **Who is affected:** Passengers on EU/UK-regulated flights, including Thai travellers flying to or from Europe. *(Thai domestic/ASEAN rules not researched.)*
- **Trigger:** A delay of 3h+, a cancellation, or denied boarding.
- **Proximate cause:** Airlines cite "extraordinary circumstances", offer vouchers, or ignore claims. Passengers give up or hire a claim agency.
- **Possible root cause (mechanism):** Incentive misalignment. The airline pays only if the passenger persists, so initial rejection is rational. Legal friction makes contingency intermediaries the default.
- **Current behavior / workaround:** Claim agencies (ES-A-10) at a 25% flat fee (ClaimFlights), 31% + €33 (EUclaim), up to 35% + 15% legal fee (others). Ryanair says up to 50% (interested party).
- **Harm:** Passengers lose 25–50% of their entitlement, or the whole of it if they never claim. AirHelp's claim that airlines reject 58% of valid claims was seen only as a headline (ITIJ, fetch 403). **Single source, unverified.**
- **Current solutions & where they fail:** Agencies fix the friction but extract a large share. A proposed 2026 EU261 reform may reduce agencies' role (snippet, details unread).
- **Who benefits / loses:** Benefits: airlines (non-claimers), agencies (fees). Loses: passengers.
- **Problem type:** Reported.
- **WTP signal:** STRONG. Passengers routinely give up 25–35%+ of their payouts, which is demonstrated willingness to pay (snippet-level, consistent across multiple comparison pages).
- **Evidence Status:** HYPOTHESIS (snippets only). **Confidence:** med.
- **Sources:** https://www.thetraveler.org/claimflights-vs-delayfix-which-service-pays-more/ (snippet); https://www.thetraveler.org/euclaim-review-after-comparing-major-flight-claim-services/ (snippet, 2026); https://www.itij.com/latest/news/airlines-reject-58-valid-passenger-compensation-claims-airhelp-says (403); https://www.theflightclub.it/en/2026/06/rivoluzione-eu261-leuropa-cambia-le-regole-sui-rimborsi-meno-spazio-alle-claim-company-piu-diritti-direttamente-ai-passeggeri/amp/ (2026-06, snippet).
- **Uncertainty:** Thai-market relevance is low unless the trip involves an EU carrier or route. The regulatory change in 2026 could shift the landscape.
- **Build-feasibility (HYPOTHESIS):** Medium. An eligibility checker plus auto-generated claim letter is buildable in 1.5 days. Actually pursuing claims (legal) is not. A heavily saturated, competitive space.

### P-A-06 — "Confirmed" accommodation bookings cancelled by the host, with OTA compensation capped below the replacement cost
- **Who is affected:** OTA users, especially in peak seasons or events.
- **Trigger:** The host or hotel cancels close to the date, plausibly when prices rise *(inference)*, or the hotel "has no record" on arrival.
- **Proximate cause:** The property cancels. The OTA offers a cheaper or lower-rated substitute or a small capped credit.
- **Possible root cause (mechanism):** Platform incentive structure. The OTA earns commission from properties and bears little cost when a property defects. Penalties on properties are weak or opaque, so the cancellation risk is pushed onto the traveler *(inference)*.
- **Current behavior / workaround:** Rebook at peak prices out of pocket. Fight support. Chargebacks *(chargebacks are inference, not sourced)*.
- **Harm (anecdotal):** Complaints describe €25 or €60 caps, a $100/booking goodwill credit vs ~€3,090 of extra cost, and being stranded at 3 a.m. in Dubai.
- **Current solutions & where they fail:** ES-A-09.
- **Who benefits / loses:** Benefits: properties that resell at higher rates (inferred). Loses: travelers.
- **Problem type:** Reported (anecdotal).
- **WTP signal:** None found.
- **Evidence Status:** HYPOTHESIS. Self-selected complaints only, with unknown prevalence. **Confidence:** low.
- **Sources:** https://www.sikayetvar.com/en/bookingcom-us/bookingcom-canceled-my-athens-apartment-and-left-me-paying-more-for-a-worse-option (2025, snippet); https://www.sikayetvar.com/en/bookingcom-us/dubai-hotel-cancelled-confirmed-booking-bookingcom-didnt-fix-it (snippet).
- **Uncertainty:** No frequency data. Mastercard noted "higher incidence of hotel booking fraud" in Phuket (snippet only), which is related but distinct.
- **Build-feasibility (HYPOTHESIS):** Low-medium. A "reconfirm directly with property" reminder/checklist is easy to build but weak. Fixing the incentive needs platform leverage.

### P-A-07 — Lost or delayed bags: airlines ignore passenger tracker data
- **Who is affected:** Checked-bag travelers, with Europe the worst region.
- **Trigger:** A bag misses a connection.
- **Proximate cause:** Airlines treat their own bag-tag system as authoritative and decline to act on AirTag locations.
- **Possible root cause (mechanism):** Information asymmetry is reversed (the passenger knows more), but there is no process or incentive for the airline to use passenger data. Liability caps limit airline cost *(liability caps are inference, not sourced here)*.
- **Current behavior / workaround:** AirTags. Social-media shaming. Find My "Share Item Location" with ~15 airlines from 2024 (ES-A-12).
- **Harm:** SITA: 6.3 mishandled bags per 1,000 passengers in 2024 (6.9 in 2023). Europe 12.3, Asia 3.1. Total ~36.2M bags (another SITA-based report says 33.4M, a discrepancy). ~US$5B cost. Air France declared a tracked bag "unrecoverable" (Sep 2025).
- **Current solutions & where they fail:** Share Item Location exists, but there are cases where the airline never opened the link.
- **Who benefits / loses:** Airlines avoid process cost. Passengers lose.
- **Problem type:** Reported.
- **WTP signal:** Passengers buy trackers (implicit). Not quantified.
- **Evidence Status:** HYPOTHESIS (SITA figures seen in multiple snippets, sita.aero not opened). **Confidence:** med.
- **Sources:** https://www.businesstraveller.com/business-travel/2025/06/13/aviation-mishandled-baggage-rates-improve-in-2024/ (2025-06-13, snippet); https://www.sita.aero/resources/surveys-reports/sita-baggage-it-insights-2024 (snippet); https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/09/16/business/airtag-checked-luggage-airline-travel (2025-09-16, snippet).
- **Uncertainty:** Asia (Thailand's region) has the lowest rate, so this is less relevant for the Thai primary market.
- **Build-feasibility (HYPOTHESIS):** Low. The fix requires airline integration. A claim-letter or evidence-pack generator is possible but thin.

### P-A-08 — Travel-insurance claims rejected over exclusions travelers didn't understand
- **Who is affected:** Insured travelers who file claims.
- **Trigger:** Medical event, cancellation or lost items. Then the claim.
- **Proximate cause:** Policy exclusions, process failures, and non-disclosure of pre-existing conditions.
- **Possible root cause (mechanism):** Product complexity and information asymmetry at purchase. Policies are sold on price, with exclusions in fine print. The buyer learns the terms only at the claim stage *(inference)*.
- **Current behavior / workaround:** Consumer guides (Which?). Complaints to the ombudsman.
- **Harm:** Which? (Apr 2024, n=882 claimants): ~16% rejected or partly paid. Top reason was exclusions (31%). 1 in 5 rejected claimants got no reason or didn't understand it. Finder AU: ~11% denial. KPMG/Pollera Canada: 95–98% paid. US Squaremouth figure of 33% denied has unverified methodology. **Figures conflict, so treat them as context.**
- **Who benefits / loses:** Insurers and comparison sites earn on sale volume. Claimants lose.
- **Problem type:** Reported.
- **WTP signal:** None found specific to "explain my policy".
- **Evidence Status:** HYPOTHESIS (snippets only). **Confidence:** med-low.
- **Sources:** https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/why-travel-insurance-goes-wrong-and-what-you-can-do-about-it-azsIq9c9qO4g (2024, snippet); https://news4jax.com/news/local/2024/09/23/why-33-of-travelers-are-being-denied-travel-insurance-claims-and-how-to-avoid-it (2024-09-23, snippet); https://www.finder.com.au/do-travel-travel-insurance-companies-pay-claims (snippet).
- **Uncertainty:** No Thailand data. Thailand has required insurance for some visa types in the past *(not verified in this scan)*.
- **Build-feasibility (HYPOTHESIS):** Medium. "Paste your policy, get plain-language exclusions" needs an LLM API, which may break the zero-budget constraint beyond free tiers. Liability risk.

### P-A-09 — Small tour operators and guides are locked into high-commission OTAs (hidden stakeholder)
- **Who is affected:** Small local operators and guides, including Thai ones. Indirectly, travelers through higher prices.
- **Trigger:** Needing discovery by international tourists.
- **Proximate cause:** Marketplaces take ~20–30%, and ranking can reportedly be bought with higher commission.
- **Possible root cause (mechanism):** Two-sided platform power. Traveler trust and reviews sit with the platform, and operators can't take repeat customers or reviews with them.
- **Current behavior / workaround:** Accept OTA fees. Use hotel concierge, touts and LINE/WhatsApp direct booking *(the latter workarounds are inference)*.
- **Harm:** Margin loss. Vulnerability to algorithm changes (vendor case: −40% in 90 days, promotional source).
- **Who benefits / loses:** Benefits: OTAs. Loses: small operators.
- **Problem type:** Reported (vendor-heavy sources).
- **WTP signal:** Operators already pay 20–30% commission for demand, which shows WTP for bookings delivered. Not for tools.
- **Evidence Status:** HYPOTHESIS. **Confidence:** low-med.
- **Sources:** https://www.phocuswire.com/viator-ben-drew-on-commission-rate-controversy-brand-building-and-2022-plans (2021, 403); https://academy.wetravel.com/getyourguide-vs-viator-comparison (snippet).
- **Uncertainty:** Little independent evidence. Commission levels for Thai operators on Klook were not verified.
- **Build-feasibility (HYPOTHESIS):** Medium for a tiny direct-booking page generator for one operator. Low for solving discovery (cold-start).

### P-A-10 — Accessibility information for lodging is unreliable
- **Who is affected:** Travelers with disabilities and wheelchair users.
- **Trigger:** Booking an "accessible" room.
- **Proximate cause:** Listings lack detail or are wrong. Accessible rooms are given away.
- **Possible root cause (mechanism):** No standardized, verified data schema. The hotel self-reports, and nobody audits.
- **Harm:** Columbia HRR (2023) survey: ~19.5% booked an accessible room and found it lacked the promised features, and ~10.3% couldn't reserve one. NPR (Dec 2025, 50 interviews + 200+ responses): rooms given away and info hard to find. MMGY: 96% of mobility-aid users faced an accommodation problem (date of report unverified).
- **WTP signal:** Open Doors: US travelers with disabilities spent ~US$50B on travel (snippet, unverified).
- **Evidence Status:** HYPOTHESIS (NPR fetch timed out, others snippet). **Confidence:** med.
- **Sources:** https://npr.org/2025/12/24/nx-s1-5564041/disabilities-wheelchairs-travel-hotels-accommodations (2025-12-24, timeout); https://datawrapper.dwcdn.net/uIAhb/7 (snippet); https://www.traveldailynews.com/?p=294558 (snippet).
- **Uncertainty:** US-centric. No Thai data.
- **Build-feasibility (HYPOTHESIS):** Medium. A crowd-verified photo and measurement checklist for a small set of Thai hotels is buildable, but the data cold-start is severe.

### P-A-11 — Opaque dual pricing for foreigners at Thai attractions
- **Who is affected:** Foreign tourists and expats (expats pay tax but are charged the foreign rate).
- **Trigger:** Paying entry at national parks or attractions.
- **Proximate cause:** Thai prices are shown in Thai numerals or hidden. Foreign prices can reportedly be 2–10× the Thai price.
- **Possible root cause (mechanism):** Policy (justified by an income gap) combined with an information asymmetry that turns a legitimate policy into a perceived scam.
- **Workaround:** The 2PriceThailand community (ES-A-08). Showing a Thai driver's licence (sometimes fails).
- **Evidence Status:** HYPOTHESIS. Sources are mostly 2020 and undated, with no 2024–25 policy news found. **Confidence:** low.
- **Sources:** https://www.nst.com.my/amp/world/region/2020/07/608136/thailand-criticised-charging-expats-more (2020-07, snippet); https://www.pattayamail.com/news/expats-and-investors-urge-government-to-address-pattaya-concerns-505809 (date n/a, snippet).
- **Build-feasibility (HYPOTHESIS):** High technically (a price-transparency page), but the harm is a perception and fairness issue, and a policy problem an app can't change. Monetization is unclear.

## Unknowns & Research Limitations
- **Reddit not retrieved directly.** Three `site:reddit.com` and Reddit-keyword searches returned news rewrites, blogs and wedding forums, never reddit.com pages. The traveler "voice" here is filtered through media rewrites. App-store reviews were reached only via aggregators (Kimola, Trustpilot snippets).
- **Blocked or failed fetches:** khaosodenglish.com (403), phocuswire.com (403), itij.com (403), npr.org (timeout), and a Splitwise help URL (404). Their content is used only at snippet level and labelled HYPOTHESIS.
- **Few primary industry reports opened.** Skift was opened but paywalled after the excerpt. Phocuswright, McKinsey, UNWTO and the SITA primary PDF were not opened (budget). The Mastercard Economics Institute primary report was not located, and the 48% figure rests on two outlets citing one report.
- **Vendor bias:** The Klook, Skyscanner, Mindtrip and KAYAK surveys are commissioned by companies selling travel. Claim-agency fee comparisons come from comparison/affiliate sites. Ryanair figures come from an interested party.
- **Geographic and language bias:** Most traveler-pain evidence is English/Western (UK Which?, US NPR/FTC, EU261). Asian data comes from Korean and Taiwanese vendor surveys. Thai-language sources (Pantip, Thai Facebook groups, Wongnai) were NOT searched, which is a key gap for the primary market.
- **Not covered due to cap:** overtourism and local resident impacts (e.g., Thai island closures), solo/female traveler safety, digital nomad visas (Thailand DTV), language barriers, Thailand's own refund/consumer rules (OCPB), and local-host perspectives beyond tour operators.
- **Stale data risk:** The Splitwise limit was verified only as of Dec 2023. The FTC junk-fee rule's status in 2026 is unverified. The dual-pricing evidence is largely from 2020.
- **Existing solutions not verified:** Other expense splitters (Tricount, Settle Up, etc.) and Thai bill-split or PromptPay group features were not checked, so the claim that no collection-or-commitment tool exists is unproven.
- **Date note:** Several search results showed dates in 2026 (e.g., Taiwan News 2026-08-11, Herald 2026-06-10). These are taken as given by the outlets.

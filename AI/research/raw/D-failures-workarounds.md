# D: Failed / Abandoned Solutions, User Workarounds and Monetization Reality

Desk research for the Phase 1 problem landscape. Compiled 2026-10-22 using about 27 WebSearch/WebFetch calls.
Scope: consumer travel first, then other consumer marketplace and social apps. Global scan with a Thailand focus. Sources from 2020 onward, except where an older case is flagged as context.

**Evidence labels**
- **FACT**: I opened the source page and the claim appears in it.
- **HYPOTHESIS (snippet-only)**: the claim appeared in search-result snippets from the named outlet, but I did not open the page. Verify before quoting it in a pitch.
- **HYPOTHESIS**: my inference from the evidence.
- **SPECULATION**: a weakly supported idea.

"Single source" marks a key number that I confirmed in only one source.

## Structural Lessons (all HYPOTHESIS, synthesized from the sources)

1. **Planning is used briefly and does not monetize; the booking is where the money is.** For more than 10 years, trip planners have died the same way: Bucket and OutTrippin (ES-D-09), then Tripnotes in the AI era (ES-D-01). The value is created at the planning step but captured by the OTA at booking (M-01, M-02). Affiliate fees are the only direct bridge, and they are small (about 4–7%) and paid late (after checkout).
2. **Low trip frequency kills subscriptions.** Tripadvisor Plus (ES-D-03) and Flight Penguin (ES-D-02) both failed with consumer subscriptions. The analyst comment that travelers "do not take enough trips" points to frequency. CleverTap's 13% week-1 repeat rate and 18% stickiness (M-07) support this.
3. **Supplier rate parity blocks "cheaper price" mechanisms.** Hotels would not undercut their own channels for Tripadvisor Plus (ES-D-03). Any mechanism that depends on suppliers offering an exclusive lower price will meet resistance unless the platform brings demand the supplier cannot get elsewhere.
4. **Subsidy and zero-commission models fail on unit economics or attract fraud.** Robinhood lost THB 2.1bn in a year at zero commission (ES-D-05). The co-pay scheme invited hotel self-attestation fraud (ES-D-07). Lesson: when a third party pays and the seller attests delivery, collusion follows. Verification of real consumption is the missing piece.
5. **Monetizing goodwill or a utility's core action causes defection.** Couchsurfing's paywall produced non-profit forks (ES-D-04, ES-D-W07). Splitwise's cap pushed users to Tricount (ES-D-10, ES-D-W05). Users treat peer coordination tools as commons.
6. **Thai coordination happens inside LINE, and payment is PromptPay or bank transfer.** The working Thai workarounds (KhunThong, bank shared pockets, per-person PromptPay QR) sit inside LINE and the banking apps (ES-D-W02 to W04). The gaps that remain are confirming who paid, nudging non-payers, and trust in a stranger or page before you transfer a deposit (ES-D-W06).
7. **Trust in social commerce is easy to fake.** Facebook page comments and appearance are the main trust signals, and scammers fake them (ES-D-W06). Peer-to-peer travel deals lack escrow, verified identity and proof of delivery. This is the same verification gap as ES-D-07.
8. **Cold start and vanity metrics.** IRL (ES-D-06) shows that social and event coordination apps can look big while the real network is tiny. A credible prototype metric is completed group actions (a trip paid and settled), not signups.

## Unknowns & Research Limitations

- **Blocked or failed fetches:** phocuswire.com returned HTTP 403, so the analyst quotes in ES-D-03 and ES-D-09 are snippet-only. affiliate.klook.com returned no usable content. Reddit and Hacker News threads did not appear in search, so there is no direct user-voice evidence from those forums.
- **Snippet-only items** (not opened): We Travel Together fraud figures, Airbnb Experiences details, Splitwise cap, Klook rates, the Booking.com 2025 restructuring, KhunThong user count, and the Facebook travel-scam cases. Open the cited pages before using any number in a pitch.
- **Conflicting numbers:** the Agoda cookie window (1 day vs ~30 days), the Splitwise daily cap (3, 4 or 10), and travel-app retention figures from different years and definitions.
- **Missing:**
  - A post-2020 founder-written post-mortem for an AI itinerary app with revenue or user numbers (Tripnotes gave none).
  - Thai-specific CAC benchmarks.
  - Real affiliate earnings for small Thai travel sites.
  - Notion travel-template sales data.
  - LINE OA pricing and message quotas, which matter for a LINE-based mechanism and were not researched here.
  - foodpanda or other Thai marketplace exits after 2024, not checked.
- **Not covered due to the search cap:** failory.com and CB Insights lists for 2020–26 consumer failures, Remote Year's 2024 shutdown details (seen in a Skift headline only), Selina and Sonder collapses, and Hostelworld or other social-travel features.
- Primary-market note: most failure evidence is Western. Thai evidence comes mainly from the Robinhood, We Travel Together, KhunThong and Facebook-scam cases.
